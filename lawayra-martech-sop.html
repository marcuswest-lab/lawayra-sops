What's Still Manual

A few things still require a human touch.

BookingLayer source data import: The "How did you hear?" source data is no longer auto-pulled by n8n. Sales tab amounts: Bookings with status "deposit_paid" show the deposit amount, not the full price. The full amount needs to be entered manually once the guest has fully paid.

New source types: If a guest writes something new that does not exist in the mapping (e.g., "AyaAdvisors"), it needs to be added to the category mapping in the Apps Script.

GG Leads sheet: This is auto-populated by Google Ads lead forms. No manual action needed — listed here for context only.

Where GG Daily's numbers come from (data lineage — read before touching)

GG Daily is filled by two separate systems, not one. Don't assume the Google Ads Script does all of it — it only does the top of the table.

  1. Spend + ad metrics (cols B–K): the Google Ads Script "Daily > Marketing Dashboard" (lives inside Google Ads account 629-914-3676 → Tools → Scripts). Pushes Date, Spend, Impressions, Clicks, Reach, CTR, CPC, Leads, CPL, Opt-in. It does not touch Booked Calls, Sales, or Revenue. (This is not n8n — the old n8n workflow E6K7ts1s0HXYw8JV is retired.)
  2. Booked Calls (col L): a nightly Apps Script (backfillMarketingDashboardV7, generalized to V8 per platform) counts appointment rows in the Sales Dashboard (1fcBghq6… Dashboard tab) per date and writes the count here.
    ⚠ Verified 2026-07-04 — col L is NOT Google-specific today. The script reads only cols A–E of the Sales Dashboard and counts every appointment row, regardless of calendar (it never checks col O). So col L currently = total booked calls across ALL calendars — Google's "La Wayra Consultation Call" plus "La Wayra Consultation Call - FB" (Meta), "Organic - LaWayra Consultation Call", "Hero Heal Consultation Call", and "La Wayra Facilitator Call". FB/organic/Hero Heal/facilitator calls are being counted as Google booked calls.
    The Google Ads consult calendar in GHL is "La Wayra Consultation Call" (appears in Sales Dashboard col O).
    Planned fix (not yet deployed): filter the count to col O = "La Wayra Consultation Call". Safe by design: the script only writes col L when the cell is empty/zero, so deploying the filter cannot change any number already in the sheet — it only affects how future days are counted, and rollback = revert the one filter line. Before deploying, confirm two things against the live sheet: (1) that "Calendar" really lives in Sales Dashboard col O, and (2) col O is populated on recent rows (older rows may be blank — a col O historical backfill would be needed to make old Google booked-call history match the new method). Marcus is OK keeping the organic bleed within the Google calendar; the point of the fix is to stop counting the other calendars (FB, Hero Heal, etc.).
  3. Sales (col N) + Revenue (col P): the same nightly Apps Script reads Sales by Source → Raw Bookings (139G…), filters to Source Category (col J) = "Google Ads" AND Status ∈ (paid, deposit_paid), then per booking date writes the count to Sales (N) and the sum of Amount USD (Raw Bookings col H) to Revenue (P). The "Google Ads" tag itself is set by update-source-category-v5.gs via the GG Leads email override (booker's email matches an email in the GG Leads sheet → tagged Google Ads regardless of what they self-reported). So Sales/Revenue is attributed by email match against the GG Leads list, not by calendar — a different, more reliable signal than Booked Calls uses. Note: the GG Leads sheet is populated automatically via Zapier (lead-form submissions routed in), not a manual Google Ads export — likely Zap 336113433. Confirm the exact trigger/source in that Zap before relying on it for a handoff.

Net: col L = appointment signal from the Sales Dashboard — currently ALL calendars, not Google-only (see red note above). cols N & P = paid-booking signal (from Raw Bookings, GG-Leads-email attributed). They measure different things and will not tie out — and until the col O filter ships, col L over-counts Google booked calls.

Section 8

Source Attribution — How It Works

How we figure out which marketing channel each booking came from, and why email matching is critical.

  1. Guest books on BookingLayer and is asked "How did you hear about us?"
  2. BookingLayer sends a daily CSV with their answers to admin@lawayra.com. An n8n workflow parses it and fills the Self-Reported Source column (col I).
  3. A separate BookingLayer report sends guest emails. Another n8n workflow parses it and fills the Email column (col F).
  4. A nightly Apps Script cross-references each guest's email against the GG Leads sheet (4,200+ Google Ads lead form emails).
  5. If the email matches a Google Ads lead → Source Category = "Google Ads" (overrides whatever they self-reported).
  6. If the guest matches a Facebook/Meta ad lead — by email or name, via the GHL "La Wayra Consultation Call - FB" calendar contacts — → Source Category = "Facebook Ads". TikTok ad leads map to "TikTok Ads".
  7. If the email does not match any paid-ad lead and they said "Google Search" → Source Category = "Organic Search/SEO".
  8. All other answers are mapped to standard categories (Friend Rec → Word of Mouth, Reddit → Reddit, etc.).
Why email matching matters: People who clicked a Google Ad often say "Google Search" when asked. Name matching misses about 57% of Google Ads bookings because people use different names on ad forms vs BookingLayer. Email is the reliable identifier.

How to Set Up a Subdomain in GHL

When you need to host a landing page or funnel on a custom subdomain (e.g., heal.lawayra.com), follow these steps to connect it through DNS and GHL.

▶ Video Walkthrough

Watch first: Loom — How to set up a subdomain in GHL — a full screen-share walking through Namecheap login, adding the CNAME, connecting the domain in GHL, and pointing it at a funnel. The written steps below mirror the video.

What You Need

Where the domain lives: Namecheap — this is where all the lawayra.com domains are registered and where you add DNS records. Login + 2FA key are in Section 8: Logins. (Hosting is on Hawkhost / cPanel, but DNS for subdomains is managed in Namecheap.)

GHL CNAME value: sites.ludicrous.cloud — this is the server our GHL account uses for all hosted pages.

Subdomain name: Choose something short and relevant to the offer (e.g., heal, community, apply, workshops).

Do NOT touch the email records. All lawayra.com email is connected to the existing MX records in Namecheap. Changing or deleting those will break email. You're only adding a new CNAME for the subdomain — leave everything else alone.

How the pieces fit together (read this first)

A subdomain is just the word in front of lawayra.com — like the heal in heal.lawayra.com. Setting one up takes two systems talking to each other:

• GHL = where the actual page lives. You tell GHL "I'm going to send people from heal.lawayra.com, get ready for them."

• Namecheap = the internet's address book for lawayra.com. You add one line (a "CNAME record") that says "when someone types heal.lawayra.com, send them to GHL's servers."

That's the whole job: tell GHL, then tell Namecheap, then have GHL double-check Namecheap. The steps below are every single click. Don't skip the green checkmark in Part 3 — that's the #1 thing people forget.

Part 1 — Pick your subdomain name

  1. Choose one short word for the front of the domain — e.g. heal, community, apply, workshops, call. Use only lowercase letters, no spaces, no symbols.
  2. Write down your full subdomain — it's your word + .lawayra.com. Example: you pick heal → your full subdomain is heal.lawayra.com. You'll type this exact thing in both GHL and Namecheap, so keep it consistent.

Part 2 — Tell GHL about the subdomain

Do this part first so GHL is ready and can confirm the record for you.

  1. Log in to GHL — go to the GHL login (see Section 8: Logins) and sign in with admin@lawayra.com. Make sure you're in the LaWayra account/location (top-left account name).
  2. Open domain settings — in the far-left menu click the Settings gear (bottom of the sidebar). In the Settings list, click Domains.
  3. Start adding the domain — click the blue Add Domain (or Connect Domain) button, usually top-right.
  4. Type your full subdomain — enter exactly what you wrote down in Part 1, e.g. heal.lawayra.com (no https://, no www, no slash). Click Continue / Add.
  5. Read the record GHL shows you — GHL now displays a CNAME record it wants you to create. It should list a Host/Name (your word, e.g. heal) and a Value/Points to of sites.ludicrous.cloud. Leave this GHL tab open — you'll come back to click Verify in Part 4.
If GHL shows a different Value than sites.ludicrous.cloud (for example preview.gohighlevel.com), trust what GHL shows on your screen and use that as the Value in Part 3. Our account normally uses sites.ludicrous.cloud because it's white-labeled.

Part 3 — Add the CNAME record in Namecheap (the DNS part)

This is the step that actually connects the address. Go slowly — it's just a form.

  1. Log in to Namecheap — go to namecheap.com and click Sign In (top-right). Enter the username and password from Section 8: Logins.
  2. Pass 2FA — Namecheap will ask for a 6-digit code. Open the 2FA browser extension (or authenticator app) noted in Section 1, copy the current 6-digit code, paste it, and continue.
  3. Open the domain list — once logged in, in the left sidebar click Domain List. You'll see lawayra.com.
  4. Click MANAGE — on the lawayra.com row, click the gray MANAGE button on the right.
  5. Open Advanced DNS — along the top you'll see tabs (Domain, Products, Sharing & Transfer, Advanced DNS). Click the Advanced DNS tab.
  6. Find the Host Records section — scroll down to the box titled HOST RECORDS. You'll see existing rows here. Do not edit or delete any of them — you're only adding one new row.
  7. Click ADD NEW RECORD — below the existing rows, click the ADD NEW RECORD button. A new blank row appears.
  8. Set the Type to CNAME — in the new row, the first column is a dropdown (it often defaults to "A Record"). Click it and choose CNAME Record.
  9. Fill in the Host — in the Host box type only your word, e.g. heal. Do NOT type heal.lawayra.com — Namecheap adds the .lawayra.com part for you automatically.
  10. Fill in the Value — in the Value (sometimes labeled "Target") box type sites.ludicrous.cloud exactly. No https://, no spaces, no trailing slash, no period at the end. (Use whatever Value GHL showed you in Part 2 if it differed.)
  11. Set TTL to Automatic — in the TTL column, leave it as Automatic (or click it and pick Automatic).
  12. SAVE the row (don't skip this!) — click the small green checkmark ✓ on the right end of the row. The row stops being editable, which means it saved. If you navigate away without clicking the checkmark, the record is lost.
  13. Sanity-check the row — it should read: Type = CNAME Record, Host = heal (your word), Value = sites.ludicrous.cloud, TTL = Automatic. Leave every other record exactly as it was.

Part 4 — Verify the connection in GHL

  1. Go back to the GHL tab from Part 2 (the one showing the CNAME record).
  2. Click Verify — click Verify Records (or Verify). If it turns green / says connected, you're done with DNS.
  3. If it fails, wait and retry — DNS changes can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few hours to spread across the internet. Wait 15–30 minutes, then click Verify again. As long as the Namecheap row is saved correctly, it will verify eventually. (You can leave and come back later.)
  4. SSL is automatic — once verified, GHL provisions the security certificate (the padlock / https) on its own. No action needed.

Part 5 — Point the subdomain at your page

  1. Open your funnel/website — in GHL's left menu go to Sites, then open the Funnel (or Website) that has the page you want to show.
  2. Open funnel Settings — inside the funnel, click the Settings tab.
  3. Set the Custom Domain — find the Domain dropdown and select the subdomain you just connected (e.g. heal.lawayra.com).
  4. Set the default path — set the page's path to / (just a slash). This makes that page load when someone visits the bare subdomain, with nothing after the .com.
  5. Save — click Save.

Part 6 — Test it

  1. Visit the subdomain — open a new browser tab and type your full subdomain, e.g. heal.lawayra.com.
  2. Check the page loads — confirm the right page appears, the padlock shows (secure https), images render, and the buttons go to the correct checkout/booking links.
  3. Test on your phone too — quickly open the same link on a phone to confirm it looks right on mobile.
Troubleshooting — if something doesn't work:
• "Not secure" warning or no page after an hour → the CNAME row probably didn't save. Go back to Namecheap → Advanced DNS and confirm the green checkmark was clicked and the row is there.
• GHL won't verify → check the Host is just your word (heal) and NOT the full heal.lawayra.com, and the Value is exactly sites.ludicrous.cloud with no extra spaces.
• Page loads but it's the wrong page → revisit Part 5 and make sure the correct page's path is set to /.
• Still stuck after a few hours → the workshops subdomain is already set up correctly — open it in Namecheap as a reference to compare against your new row.
Important notes:
• Our GHL uses sites.ludicrous.cloud as the CNAME value — not the default preview.gohighlevel.com. This is because the account is white-labeled.
• The workshops subdomain is already set up this way and can be used as a reference.
• If pasting custom HTML into GHL, you may need to add CSS overrides to remove GHL's default container margins and borders. The /build-landing-page skill includes these overrides automatically.
• SSL certificates are provisioned automatically by GHL once the domain is verified.

This is the automated daily email sequence that goes out to the LaWayra list from inside GHL — separate from the one-off Drip broadcasts in Section 19. Emails are written under Sam Believ's voice and signature, drafted with a custom GPT, reviewed, then loaded into the GHL sequence one at a time.

How this works at a glance: The sequence sends one email per day to everyone on the list. The goal is to build it out to ~180 emails total. You draft them in batches of roughly 5–10 using the GPT, keep one master document of everything written so far, get the batch reviewed, then load each approved email into the live GHL workflow one at a time.

Part A — Drafting the Emails

  1. Keep one master document of all uploaded emails. There is a running TextEdit (or Google Doc) file containing every email already written. Each time you draft, you re-feed this whole file to the GPT so it doesn't repeat hooks, stories, or quotes. Save the file with the current date in the name (e.g. LW Emails 1-20-26) so you always know which version is latest.
  2. Re-upload the master file to the GPT first. Open the LW Emails GPT → Edit GPT → Configure → under Knowledge, remove the old file and upload the latest dated master file. This refreshes what the GPT "knows" has already been sent. Save the GPT.
  3. Ask for the next batch by number. Prompt it explicitly, e.g. "Please write me the next five emails" or "Write emails 41–45." Working in small numbered batches keeps numbering consistent with the master doc and the GHL sequence.
  4. Fact-check every name, quote, and claim. The GPT hallucinates — it invents guest names, fabricates podcast guests, and reuses quotes. For anything specific, challenge it directly (e.g. "Has Dennis McKenna really been on the podcast?") and verify before trusting it. When in doubt, cut the detail.
  5. Ask it to elaborate where emails feel thin. If a draft is too short or shallow, prompt it to lengthen and add depth. Keep Sam Believ's warm, first-person voice — not the brand marketing voice.
  6. Paste approved drafts into the master doc and format them. Add each email to the doc, format the email's title/subject as Heading 3, and label them in sequence (Email 41, Email 42…) so they appear in the document sidebar and stay easy to navigate. Delete any GPT commentary or chatter — only the clean email body should remain.
  7. Mark new drafts as "not uploaded yet." Keep a clear separator in the doc between emails already loaded into GHL and the fresh batch waiting to go in. This is your bookkeeping so nothing gets uploaded twice or skipped.
  8. Send the batch for review. Share with Marcus before anything goes into GHL. Start small, get the format approved, then continue.

Part B — Uploading to the GHL Sequence

⚠ Be careful — this workflow is live. The email sequence is an active workflow with real contacts moving through it daily. A mistake here goes out to the whole list. Work slowly and double-check each email before saving.
  1. Get access. The GHL invitation arrives from "Lead Connector (no reply)". Accept it to get into the sub-account.
  2. Open the sequence workflow. In the sub-account go to Automation → open the "new email subscriber workflow" folder → open "email sequence" (the first one). This is the active sequence — confirm you're in the right workflow before editing.
  3. Find the last email and duplicate its block. Scroll to the bottom of the workflow to the final "Wait one day" → "Send email" pair. Click the three dots on the last "Wait one day" step → "Copy all actions from here" → then "Paste below." This clones the wait + email structure so you keep the daily cadence intact.
  4. Renumber the new email. The pasted action will say e.g. "Send email 40." Rename it to the next number (41, 42…) so it matches your master doc.
  5. Set the subject. Pull the subject line from the Copy of LW Emails doc and paste it in.
  6. Paste the body and personalize the greeting. Paste the email body, then replace the greeting with the dynamic variable for the contact's first name: {{contact.first_name}}. Use the exact variable syntax — a typo means it renders blank.
  7. Match the formatting to email 40. Set the font to Verdana, size 16px so it's consistent with the rest of the sequence.
  8. ⚠ Add the unsubscribe link at the very bottom of EVERY email. This is the most important step. Missing unsubscribe links hurt email health and land you in spam. The unsubscribe section sometimes fails to paste or save — so after adding it, double-check it's actually there before moving on.
  9. Save properly. Click Save Action, then Save on the workflow. If you don't save, GHL silently reverts your changes — always confirm the save took.
  10. Update your bookkeeping. Back in the master doc, move the email you just uploaded from the "not uploaded yet" section into the uploaded section and delete the separator. Repeat for the next email.
  11. Spot-check. Send yourself a test and confirm formatting, the first-name variable, links, and the unsubscribe footer all render correctly.
Quality rules (learned the hard way):
• Never use real retreat-guest or reviewer names. The GPT keeps inserting actual patient/guest or Google-reviewer names — replace these with "one guest" or "this guest said…" to protect their privacy. Manually scrub every batch. (Podcast guest names are fine — those are public figures who appeared on the show. The rule is only about private retreat guests/reviewers.)
• ⚠ Hallucinated quotes and podcast guests are the #1 problem — check EVERY one. This happens constantly. The GPT routinely fabricates podcast guests who were never on the show and invents or reuses quotes that were never said. Treat every named guest and every quotation mark as guilty until proven innocent: confirm the guest actually appeared on the podcast, and confirm any quote is real, before it goes anywhere near GHL. If you can't verify it, cut it.
• Link the exact podcast episode. If an email references a podcast episode, link directly to that Spotify episode — don't make people dig through 130+ episodes.
• Unsubscribe link in every single email. No exceptions — it's the difference between landing in the inbox and landing in spam.
• Daily cadence is intentional. Sending daily is best practice here; open rates run 30–50%. Don't reduce frequency on a hunch.
• Always get a review before a batch goes live in the sequence.
